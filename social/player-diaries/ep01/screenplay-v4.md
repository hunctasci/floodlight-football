# 48 Hours Before Belgium — screenplay v4 (DRAFT for owner review)

Owner decisions (2026-10-01): **the father has passed away, said once** · **interviewer questions are on-screen `Q:` cards (no interviewer voice)** · **male voice: audition fictional voices, owner picks by ear** before any re-voicing.

## 1. What v3 is actually telling the viewer (the honest read)

Watched cold, as a stranger scrolling, v3 says this:

> A phone. A family chat, a man called Lucas. Black. A woman asks "Are you nervous?" A man drives at
> night: "Ask Belgium." A title. Suddenly it is morning, a child says he's late (late for what?). A man
> thinks about his father. A woman asks about his diet (is that his wife?). His wife takes his coffee. He
> drives. An old man wants two goals. Football training. He plays with his son. Dinner needs salt. Now he
> is in an interview chair (there was an interview?). His son sleeps. A tunnel. "Ask Belgium." Logo.
> Coach wants to see him.

The pictures are fine. **The viewer can't follow it**, for six reasons, worst first:

1. **Three voices from the same man, and nobody's mouth moves.** #9 speaks on camera, #9 thinks in
   voice-over, and he answers an off-camera interviewer. With mouthless characters and one timbre, the
   viewer can't tell speech from thought from interview.
2. **An interviewer nobody has met.** A woman's voice asks questions from the very first second, but the
   interview isn't seen until 80 s in. In the kitchen her voice is heard *next to the partner*, so
   viewers think the partner is asking "Special diet before Belgium?"
3. **Time jumps backwards.** 06:46 morning (chat) → night drive → 06:47 morning. "48 hours" is promised
   but never counted.
4. **No dramatic question the viewer is holding.** "Are you nervous?" is asked, but the film gives us
   nothing to watch *for*. Scenes are joined by "and then", not "but" or "so".
5. **Characters with no job.** Lucas (one DM, never seen), Mum (never seen), the interviewer (unseen). The
   kid's "You're late" has no object. Late for what?
6. **The father is mentioned three times and never explained.** Is he alive? Was he a player? It feels
   like it matters, but the viewer can't tell why.

## 2. v4 — the rewrite

**Logline.** Two days before Türkiye play Belgium, the country's #9 tells everyone he isn't nervous. His
seven-year-old son knows better.

**Theme.** What fathers pass on. Three generations:
- his father, who played and is gone
- him
- his son, who copies everything he does

**The spine (one question, asked at 0:04, answered at the last line).**
- Mum texts *"are you nervous??"*. He types *"ask belgium."*, deletes it, and sends *"no."*
- His son texts: *"he is"*.
- Everything after that is the film proving the son right, and showing us why that's fine.

**One narrator, clearly marked.**
- **The diary is his voice-over.** It's dated, it's the only "inside" voice, and it's set as italic captions.
- **There is no interviewer voice.** The documentary questions appear as on-screen text cards
  (`Q: SPECIAL DIET BEFORE BELGIUM?`), then he answers on camera. That's the native Reels Q&A format,
  and it's on-screen text, which we know performs. It also removes the second female voice next to
  the partner.
- **Day/time stamps** carry the countdown: WEDNESDAY 06:47 … FRIDAY 20:45 · BRUSSELS.

**Cut for clarity:** Lucas and the night-drive open. The unsent *"ask belgium."* in the chat now sets
up the tunnel callback: what he couldn't send, he finally says out loud.
